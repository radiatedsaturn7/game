Laughing in the Abyss - Game Design Document

Overview

Laughing in the Abyss is a co-op survival horror board game for two players: Robtergeist and Cait Vex. Trapped in a decaying digital void, you and your companion wander through a shifting labyrinth of despair, dread, and malfunctioning memories. Your only hope is to make it out together. The abyss laughs—not because it’s happy, but because it’s gone mad.

Game Objective

Navigate the ever-changing 5x5 grid (expandable to 7x7 if needed for balance) while managing Sanity and shared Hope. Discover the Exit Tile, complete the required cinematic Escape Encounter, and survive—together. If either player is lost to madness, or Hope drops to zero, the game ends.

Roles

Robtergeist – A delusional, eccentric scientist clinging to reason.

Cait Vex – The hardened skeptic who calls bullshit, but never leaves your side.

Core Stats

1. Sanity (per player)

Max: 10

If a player hits 0: That player is lost to the Abyss. Game over.

2. Hope (shared)

Max: 10

If Hope reaches 0: All is lost. Game over.

Grid Layout

Default: 5x5 tile grid (expandable to 7x7 or variable shapes).

Each tile may contain:

A Location (persistent effect)

An optional Encounter (one-time event)

Or both.

Players begin at a random revealed tile. The Exit Tile is hidden and can only be revealed under specific conditions (e.g., all corners visited, key items found, etc.).

Turns

Each turn, players can:

Move (N/S/E/W)

Use an item

Trade items (if on same tile)

Use a card before/after movement or during encounter resolution

Rest (optional – see Resting below)

Movement Rules

Players may be together or separate.

Movement into a new tile reveals the tile’s contents.

Moving onto the same tile allows trading and some encounter benefits.

Locations vs. Encounters: Clarified

Locations

Locations are persistent environmental effects tied to a tile. They represent the ongoing nature or aura of a place in the Abyss. Once revealed, the Location effect becomes active and remains in play permanently.

Triggered when: A player enters, ends a turn on, or interacts with the tile.

Examples:

"Hall of Broken Echoes" – Any time you speak on this tile, lose 1 Sanity.

"Sanctuary Node" – Resting here restores +1 Sanity.

Anti-Camping Mechanic: To discourage camping, many beneficial Locations apply diminishing returns, trigger Abyssal Shadows if a player lingers too long, or impose a Hope tax. Example: “Sanctuary Node – Restore +1 Sanity, but -1 Hope if you end more than one turn in a row here.” Shadows may appear after 2+ consecutive turns and force a sanity check or confrontation.

Encounters

Encounters are one-time, narrative-driven events that occur when a tile is first revealed. Think of them as the Abyss reacting to your presence.

Triggered when: A player moves into an unrevealed tile that includes an Encounter.

After resolution: The Encounter is discarded; only the Location remains (if there is one).

Not every Location has an Encounter. Encounters are somewhat rare and impactful—designed to shake things up and make the world feel alive (or cursed).

With only 25 tiles in the base grid, maybe 8–12 have Encounters. This keeps the pacing tense but not overwhelming. Larger grids scale that ratio up.

Effects Index

Here’s a full list of the types of effects used in the game:

1. Sanity Effects (Individual)

Increase/Decrease by X

Temporary immunity to loss

Sanity check: Roll 1d6; if <X, suffer consequence

2. Hope Effects (Shared)

Increase/Decrease by X

Hope resonance: Split Hope loss/gain between players

Hope inversion: Swap Hope and lowest Sanity

3. Reveal Mechanics

Reveal adjacent tiles

Reveal diagonal tiles

Reveal a tile chosen by player

Reveal tile farthest from current position

4. Tile Manipulation

Rotate row or column

Swap tile positions

Lock/unlock tiles

Add barriers or open pathways

5. Dice Roll Checks

Standard: Roll 1d6

Success thresholds vary by effect

May be modified by items, environment, or sanity

6. Item-Based Effects

Gain item

Lose item

Trade item with other player

Use item to cancel or modify effect

7. Time/Turn-Based Triggers

Effect every X turns

Countdown tiles (e.g., "Collapses in 3 turns")

8. Teleportation / Movement Effects

Forced movement to another tile

Swap player positions

Return to starting tile

Pull other player toward you

Proximity Mechanics: Sticking Together vs. Splitting Up

Sticking Together

Benefits:

Can trade, protect, or support each other.

Some encounters give bonuses if both players are present.

Final Escape Encounters require both players.

Drawbacks:

Risk of shared damage or effects.

Reveals fewer tiles per turn.

Some locations punish clustering (e.g., "Claustrophobic Pressure").

Splitting Up

Benefits:

Cover more ground, find the exit faster.

Trigger multiple location benefits.

Gain unique perspectives from different parts of the board.

Drawbacks:For

Can't trade or help each other.

Increased chance of encountering danger solo.

Harder to reunite late game if the board is large.

Balancing Mechanic Ideas:

Echo Tiles: Allow communication or buffs between distant players.

Tethering Items: Let one player pull the other to them once per game.

Shared Visions: If players are on mirrored tiles, both gain +1 Sanity.

Midgame Shakeup: Abyssal Shift

At a set point (e.g., after 10 turns or revealing 12 tiles), the board triggers an Abyssal Shift:

Reroll or shuffle rows/columns

Replace some tiles with corrupted versions

Sanity storm: Both players must roll for Sanity loss

This keeps pacing dynamic and forces adaptation.

Player Progression & Replayability

Players may find Key Items, which unlock paths, cancel effects, or alter the final encounter.

Add a Memory System: Some encounters leave permanent quirks/boons/flaws that persist across turns.

Add optional Character Traits (e.g., Robtergeist is immune to certain effects but more vulnerable to others).

Use Game Modifiers at start (e.g., “Abyssal Rule: No Sanity can be recovered this game.”)

Resting

May be done instead of moving.

Resting improves Sanity but drains Hope—unless you're in a Sanctuary.

Resting on a normal tile: +1 Sanity, -1 Hope.

Resting on a Sanctuary tile: +1 Sanity, no Hope loss.

Rest Cooldown: Players may not rest two turns in a row.

Game End Conditions

Hope reaches 0 → Game over.

A player’s Sanity reaches 0 → Game over.

Both players reach the Escape tile and complete its final Encounter → Victory.

Final Escape Encounter

One randomly drawn from the Cinematic End Deck.

Requires both players on the Exit Tile.

The encounter includes a layered final trial composed of:

A dice challenge – Players must roll against thresholds influenced by combined Sanity, Hope, or specific items.

A sanity sync mechanic – Sanity disparity between players can lead to penalties, increased difficulty, or alternate outcomes.

Item-based options – Players may sacrifice or use collected Key Items to affect results, skip rolls, or unlock alternate paths.

Memory-based boosts – Specific past events (like "Echo of the Elevator") provide bonuses or narrative advantages.

Optional morality decision – These occur before or just as the final encounter is triggered, and are designed to be fast, clear, and emotionally resonant. Examples include:

"You hear a familiar voice calling. Follow it and both players lose 1 Sanity. Ignore it and lose 2 Hope."

"One of you sees a version of the exit that looks 'too good to be true.' That player can choose to step in alone. If they do, the other must face the encounter alone—at double difficulty."

"You both must agree to discard your last held items. If either refuses, the final encounter becomes corrupted and rolls require +1 difficulty."

These morality moments inject tension and choice without bogging down the ending. They serve as narrative punctuation—a last whisper from the Abyss asking what kind of survivors you really are.

Closing Notes

This game isn’t about power—it’s about crawling through despair with someone who never left your side. Laughing in the Abyss is a shared scream into the dark. The world out there may be broken, but down here, at least you're not alone.
